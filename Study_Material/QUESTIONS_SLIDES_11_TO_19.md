### Q10. [EQB-11-10] Scuba-Diving Power Plant CO2 Scrubbing: BIG-Bicarbonate Technology
- **Marks:** `2M – 3M`

- **Importance:** MEDIUM-HIGH (⭐⭐⭐)

**Question as on Question Paper:**
> Explain how carbon-capture technology scrubs CO2 from power plants like scuba-diving gear using BIG-bicarbonate solids. Describe the capture, heating, release, and condensation process.

<details>
<summary><b>View Model Answer Script (2M – 3M)</b></summary>

**Exam Calibrated Answer: 2M – 3M**
**[For 2 Marks — Two-Stage Amine Scrubbing Process (1M each)]:**
- **Absorption Stage (1 Mark):** Cooled flue gas containing $\text{CO}_2$ enters the bottom of an absorption column and flows upward against a descending aqueous liquid amine solvent (typically 30% Monoethanolamine - MEA). The basic amine reacts exothermically with weak acidic $\text{CO}_2$ at $40\text{--}50^{\circ}\text{C}$ to form soluble carbamate salts, scrubbing $>90\%$ of $\text{CO}_2$ from the exhaust gas.
- **Desorption / Stripping Stage (1 Mark):** The $\text{CO}_2$-rich amine solvent is pumped into a stripper column and heated to $120\text{--}140^{\circ}\text{C}$ using steam reboilers. The thermal energy breaks the chemical bonds, liberating high-purity ($\sim 99\%$) $\text{CO}_2$ gas for compression and underground geological storage, while the regenerated solvent is recycled back to the absorber.
**[If asked for 3 Marks, add Energy Penalty & Challenges (1M)]:**
The solvent regeneration requires massive thermal parasitic energy ($\sim 3.0\text{ to }4.0\text{ GJ/tonne CO}_2$), reducing power plant net electricity output by 20–30%, alongside solvent thermal degradation and equipment corrosion.

</details>

### Q1. [EQB-16-01] System of National Accounts (SNA) Framework & Historical Popularity
- **Marks:** `5M`
- **Question Type / Format:** Descriptive & Case Study
- **Importance:** CRITICAL (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
- **Exam Appearances:** End Exam 2024 (Section II, Q1 [5M]); End Exam 2023 (Section IV, Q1 [5M])

**Question as on Question Paper:**
> What is SNA? Define its framework? Why did the concept of SNA remain so popular for so long despite ignoring the environment?

<details>
<summary><b>View Model Answer Script (5M)</b></summary>

**Exam Calibrated Answer: 5 Marks**
**1. Definition & UN Framework (1.5 Marks):**
<p>The **System of National Accounts (SNA)** is the standardized international statistical accounting framework developed by the United Nations, World Bank, and IMF to measure macroeconomic economic activity, output, and national income through aggregate metrics like Gross National Product (GNP):
$$\text{GNP} = C + I + G + (X - M) + (R - P)$$
where $C$ is consumption, $I$ is gross private investment, $G$ is government expenditure, $(X - M)$ is net exports, and $(R - P)$ is net factor receipts from abroad.</p>
**2. Why SNA Remained Popular Historically [3.5 Marks (Write any 4 reasons, ~1M each)]:**
- **Objective Market Transaction Base:** Measures only observable monetary transactions with clear price tags, eliminating subjective or non-monetary valuation disputes.
- **Standardized International Comparison:** Provides uniform rules allowing cross-country benchmarking of economic growth, creditworthiness, and international treaty standing.
- **Direct Policy Alignment:** Directly correlates with tax revenue generation, fiscal deficits, and employment targeting.
- **Simplicity via Ignored Externalities:** Treats natural resources as free gifts of nature, making computation straightforward by ignoring environmental depletion and pollution degradation costs.

</details>

### Q3. [EQB-16-03] Hicksian Income and Green Accounting
- **Marks:** `2M`
- **Question Type / Format:** Short Answer & Definitions
- **Importance:** CRITICAL (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
- **Exam Appearances:** End Exam 2024 (Section III, Q(b) [2M]); End Exam 2022 (Section V, Q(a) [2M])

**Question as on Question Paper:**
> Relate Hicksian income with Green accounting.

<details>
<summary><b>View Model Answer Script (2M)</b></summary>

**Exam Calibrated Answer: 2 Marks (1 Mark each)**
<li>**Hicksian Income Definition (1 Mark):** Proposed by economist Sir John Hicks, true income ($Y_H$) is the maximum amount an individual or nation can consume during a time period while still expecting to be as well off at the end of the period as at the beginning:
$$Y_H = \text{Consumption} + \Delta K \quad (\text{subject to } \Delta K \ge 0)$$
It demands that capital stock ($K$) must not decline.</li>
<li>**Relation to Green Accounting (1 Mark):** Conventional GDP violates Hicksian principles by counting liquidations of natural capital ($K_n$) as current income rather than asset depletion. Green Accounting operationalizes Hicksian sustainability by deducting both manufactured depreciation ($D_m$) and natural capital depletion ($D_n$) to derive **Environmentally Adjusted Domestic Product (EDP)**:
$$\text{EDP} = \text{GDP} - D_m - D_n$$
ensuring national consumption does not erode the natural capital base.</li>

</details>

### Q5. [EQB-16-05] The Three Market Axioms and Market Failure for Environmental Goods
- **Marks:** `3M`
- **Question Type / Format:** Descriptive & Case Study
- **Importance:** CRITICAL (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
- **Exam Appearances:** End Exam 2023 (Section II, Q1 [3M]); End Exam 2019 (Section III, Q2 [3M])

**Question as on Question Paper:**
> "No Transaction: No Existence; No Money: No Existence; No Value Judgment: No Existence." Explain how the three market axioms lead to market failure for environmental goods.

<details>
<summary><b>View Model Answer Script (3M)</b></summary>

**Exam Calibrated Answer: 3 Marks (1 Mark each)**
**Explanation of Market Axioms and Environmental Market Failure:**
- **Excludability Axiom (1 Mark):** A seller can prevent non-payers from consuming the good. Environmental public goods (clean air, ocean fisheries) are *non-excludable*; free-riders cannot be excluded, leading to zero market prices.
- **Rivalry Axiom (1 Mark):** One person's consumption reduces availability for others. Many environmental regulatory services (climate regulation, ozone layer) are *non-rival*; my breathing clean air does not diminish air availability for another.
- **Market Failure & The Quote's Meaning (1 Mark):** Conventional markets register "existence" only when an explicit price tag and monetary transaction occur. Because environmental public goods lack property rights, market prices, and financial transactions, the conventional economic system treats them as having zero economic value, leading to unregulated overexploitation and catastrophic degradation.

</details>

### Q9. [EQB-16-09] Genuine Progress Indicator (GPI) vs. GDP
- **Marks:** `5M`
- **Question Type / Format:** Descriptive & Case Study
- **Importance:** CRITICAL (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
- **Exam Appearances:** End Exam 2024 (Section II, Q3 [5M]); End Exam 2022 (Section IV, Q(b) [5M])

**Question as on Question Paper:**
> How is GPI (Genuine Progress Indicator) created? What is its significance compared to GDP?

<details>
<summary><b>View Model Answer Script (5M)</b></summary>

**Exam Calibrated Answer: 5 Marks**
**1. Formulation of Genuine Progress Indicator (GPI) [2 Marks]:**
<p>GPI replaces GDP by starting with Personal Consumption Expenditures adjusted for income inequality, then adding non-market benefits and subtracting social/environmental damages:
$$\text{GPI} = C_{\text{adj}} + G_{\text{non-market}} - D_{\text{defensive}} - E_{\text{environmental}} - S_{\text{social}}$$</p>
**2. Core Adjustments & Significance [3 Marks (1M each)]:**
- **Income Distribution Adjustment (1M):** Divides consumption expenditure by an income inequality coefficient (Gini); growth that benefits only the rich reflects lower welfare gains.
- **Deduction of Defensive Expenditures (1M):** Unlike GDP which counts oil spill cleanup costs, medical bills, and accident repairs as positive economic growth, GPI treats them as defensive costs and deducts them.
- **Threshold Hypothesis (Max-Neef) (1M):** Beyond a certain level of GDP per capita, the human and environmental costs of additional growth exceed the benefits, causing GPI to decouple and decline even as conventional GDP continues to rise.

</details>

- **Marks:** `5M`

**Question as on Question Paper:**
> Explain surrogate market approach methods with examples.

<details>
<summary><b>View Model Answer Script (5M)</b></summary>

**Exam Calibrated Answer: 5 Marks (2.5 Marks each)**
**Two Core Surrogate Market Approach Methods:**
<li>**Hedonic Pricing Method (HPM) (2.5 Marks):**
    <li>*Premise & Formula (1.5M):* Deconstructs the market price of a private asset (typically residential property) into its structural attributes ($S$), neighborhood features ($N$), and environmental quality attributes ($E$):
    $$P_{\text{property}} = f(S_1, \dots, S_n, N_1, \dots, N_m, E_{\text{air}}, E_{\text{noise}}, E_{\text{green}})$$</li>
    - *Example (1M):* Comparing two identical apartments where one overlooks an urban park or quiet lake while the other faces a noisy highway. The price premium of the park-facing property reveals consumers' implicit Willingness to Pay (WTP) for peace and clean air.
</li>
<li>**Travel Cost Method (TCM) (2.5 Marks):**
    <li>*Premise & Formula (1.5M):* Evaluates the economic recreational value of non-priced natural ecosystems (national parks, wildlife sanctuaries) based on visitors' travel expenses:
    $$V = f(C_t, T_o, S_i, Y_i)$$
    where $C_t$ is direct travel cost (fuel, transit fares), $T_o$ is opportunity cost of time, $S_i$ is substitute sites, and $Y_i$ is income.</li>
    - *Example (1M):* Estimating the recreational value of Jim Corbett National Park by surveying visitors' total travel expenditures, creating a surrogate demand curve to determine total consumer surplus.
</li>

</details>

**Question as on Question Paper:**
> What is the difference between strong and weak sustainability? Discuss the capital substitutability assumption, critical natural capital, and explain under which category Green Accounting is placed and why.

<details>
<summary><b>View Model Answer Script (2M – 4M)</b></summary>

**Exam Calibrated Answer: 2M – 4M**
**[For 2 Marks — Two Core Differences (1M each)]:**
  Parameter
  Weak Sustainability (Solow / Hartwick)
  Strong Sustainability (Daly / Costanza)
  **Substitutability (1M)**
  Assumes natural capital ($K_n$) and manufactured capital ($K_m$) are **perfect substitutes**. Total capital stock ($K = K_m + K_n$) must remain non-declining.

  **Ecological Outlook (1M)**

  Ecological realism: natural systems exhibit thermodynamic limits, non-linearities, tipping points, and irreversible extinction.

  **2**
  **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act**
  **1974**
  Established Central and State Pollution Control Boards (CPCB/SPCBs) to regulate industrial water discharge.
  **3**
  **Forest (Conservation) Act**
  **1980**
  Mandates prior Central Government approval before diverting any forest land for non-forest industrial purposes.
  **4**
  **Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act**
  **1981**
  Empowered SPCBs to designate Air Pollution Control Areas and regulate industrial stack emission limits (noise added 1987).
  **5**
  **Environment (Protection) Act**
  **1986**
  Umbrella legislation enacted post-Bhopal Gas Tragedy (1984) empowering the Central Govt to set standards and issue EIA/CRZ rules.
