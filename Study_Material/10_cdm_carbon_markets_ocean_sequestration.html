Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) & Ocean Sequestration

Kyoto Flexible Instruments, Additionality Rubrics, and Oceanic Carbon Injection
💡 Why This Lecture Matters: The Clean Development Mechanism (Article 12 of the Kyoto Protocol) pioneered international market-based carbon finance, enabling Annex I developed nations to invest in emission-reduction projects in developing nations in exchange for Certified Emission Reductions (CERs). Concurrently, the ocean—Earth's largest active carbon pool—has been investigated for ocean iron fertilization and direct liquid $CO_2$ injection, raising complex ecological questions regarding ocean acidification and marine food web collapse.

Deep Dive

Topic Synthesis: UNFCCC, Kyoto Protocol Framework & Certified Emission Reductions (CERs)

1. UNFCCC Foundations & CBDR Principle (Slides 3–4): Adopted at the 1992 Rio Earth Summit. The cornerstone principle is Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (CBDR): recognizes that developed nations (Annex I) produced the vast majority of historical cumulative emissions and must take the lead in mitigation, while developing nations (non-Annex I) have overriding priorities of poverty eradication and economic development.

2. Kyoto Protocol (1997 / Ratified 2005, Slides 6–8): Established legally binding emission reduction targets for Annex I nations (-5.2% below 1990 levels during 2008–2012). It created three market-based flexible mechanisms: Clean Development Mechanism (CDM), Joint Implementation (JI), and International Emissions Trading (IET).

3. The Six Greenhouse Gases Covered (Slide 11): Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$, GWP=1), Methane ($CH_4$, GWP=28), Nitrous Oxide ($N_2O$, GWP=265), Hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs), Perfluorocarbons (PFCs), and Sulfur Hexafluoride ($SF_6$, GWP=23,500).

4. Concept of Certified Emission Reductions (CERs, Slides 9–10): $1\,\text{CER} = 1\,\text{metric tonne of } CO_2\text{-equivalent}$ avoided or sequestered. An Annex I country invests in a green project in a non-Annex I nation, earning CERs to offset its domestic compliance cap at lower marginal cost.

Governing Quantitative Model
$$1\,\text{CER} = 1\,\text{tCO}_2\text{e} = \sum_{i} m_i \cdot GWP_i \quad | \quad \text{Kyoto Target: } -5.2\% \text{ below 1990 levels}$$
⚠️
Midsem/Endsem Exam Checkpoint for These Slides:

State the definition of 1 CER (1 metric tonne of CO2 equivalent). Name all six greenhouse gases covered under Annex A of the Kyoto Protocol and their relative GWPs.

Deep Dive

Topic Synthesis: CDM Additionality Rules, Renewable Energy & Powerguda Village Case

1. The Additionality Principle (Slide 16): The cardinal rule of the CDM: a project is 'additional' if and only if anthropogenic emissions are reduced below those that would have occurred in the absence of the certified project activity (the baseline scenario). The project must overcome financial, technological, or institutional barriers that would otherwise prevent its execution without carbon finance.

2. Eligible Project Categories (Slides 17–22): Renewable energy (solar, wind, small hydro, tidal), industrial fuel switching, industrial fugitive methane capture from landfills, and agricultural soil management (e.g. replacing urea with ammonium sulfate: 1 ton of urea emits ~0.73 tons of $CO_2$ during hydrolysis).

3. Powerguda Village Landmark Case Study (July 2004, Slide 23): Powerguda, a remote tribal village in Adilabad District, Andhra Pradesh, became the first village in India to sell carbon credits directly to the World Bank. Villagers planted 4,500 Pongamia pinnata (karanja) trees on degraded land, extracting non-edible oil to produce biodiesel that displaced diesel fuel in municipal generators, earning $645 in verified carbon finance.

4. Transition to Paris Agreement Article 6 (Slide 24): In 2015, the Paris Agreement replaced Kyoto's top-down targets with nationally determined contributions (NDCs). Article 6 formalizes new bilateral and multilateral carbon market mechanisms (ITMOs - Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes).

Governing Quantitative Model
$$\text{Emission Reductions: } ER_y = BE_y - PE_y - LE_y \quad (\text{Baseline} - \text{Project} - \text{Leakage})$$
⚠️

Deep Dive

Topic Synthesis: Azolla Sequestration, Geological Storage & The Ocean Iron Hypothesis

1. Biological Sequestration via Azolla (Slide 28): Floating water fern *Azolla filiculoides* displays exceptional carbon capture: sequesters 32.54 metric tonnes $CO_2$/ha/year after only 18 days of growth with 10% initial cover, due to its symbiotic cyanobacterium *Anabaena azollae* fixing atmospheric nitrogen at high rates.

2. Geological Sequestration & The Illinois Basin Project (Slides 29–30): Injecting supercritical $CO_2$ ($T > 31.1^\circ\text{C}, P > 73.9\,\text{bar}$, exhibiting liquid-like density and gas-like viscosity) into deep saline formations at depths $>800$ meters beneath impermeable caprock shales. The Illinois Basin - Decatur Project (IBDP, 2007–2021) safely injected over 1 million metric tons of anthropogenic $CO_2$ into the Mount Simon Sandstone.

3. John Martin's Ocean Iron Hypothesis (1988, Slides 31–32): Oceanographer John Martin observed High-Nutrient, Low-Chlorophyll (HNLC) ocean regions (Southern Ocean, sub-Arctic Pacific) where abundant nitrate and phosphate remain unutilized by phytoplankton due to micronutrient iron limitation. Martin famously stated: 'Give me half a tanker of iron, and I will give you an ice age.' Adding ferrous iron salts ($FeSO_4$) stimulates massive diatom blooms that draw down atmospheric $CO_2$.

Governing Quantitative Model
$$CO_2 \text{ Supercritical State: } T_c = 31.1^\circ\text{C}, \quad P_c = 7.39\,\text{MPa} \implies \rho \approx 600 - 800\,\text{kg/m}^3$$
⚠️
Midsem/Endsem Exam Checkpoint for These Slides:

State the critical temperature and pressure for supercritical CO2 ($31.1^\circ\text{C}, 73.9\,\text{bar}$). Know John Martin's Iron Hypothesis and what 'HNLC' stands for (High-Nutrient, Low-Chlorophyll).

Topic Synthesis: Ocean Fertilization Verification Hazards, Toxic Diatoms & Deep Anoxia

1. Ocean Fertilization Verification Crisis (Slide 38): International scientific consensus holds that commercial carbon credits for ocean fertilization cannot be reliably verified. To evaluate net carbon drawdown, the project boundary must encompass the entire global ocean; lateral advection transports sequestered biomass, and zooplankton respiration returns >90% of captured carbon back to the atmosphere as dissolved inorganic carbon before it sinks past the 1,000m lysocline.

2. Toxic Phytoplankton Proliferation (Slides 39–41): In situ iron addition experiments (such as the 2007 Sulu Sea urea experiment) often stimulate undesirable species rather than beneficial large diatoms:

  • Dinoflagellate Blooms: Produce saxitoxins responsible for fatal Paralytic Shellfish Poisoning (PSP).
  • Pseudo-nitzschia Diatoms: Produce neurotoxin domoic acid, which causes Amnesic Shellfish Poisoning (ASP), bioaccumulating up marine food webs to cause mortality in sea lions, whales, and humans.

3. Deep Ocean Anoxia & Nutrient Robbing (Slide 42): Sinking biomass decomposition consumes dissolved oxygen in intermediate water layers, expanding mid-water Oxygen Minimum Zones (OMZs) and triggering production of potent greenhouse gases ($N_2O, CH_4$).

Governing Quantitative Model
$$\text{Redfield-Brzezinski Stoichiometry: } 106\text{C} : 16\text{N} : 1\text{P} : 0.001\text{Fe} \quad | \quad \text{Export Efficiency } \eta < 5\%$$

Deep Dive

Topic Synthesis: Ocean Acidification, Coal-Bed Methane Displacement & Novacem Cement

1. Ocean Acidification Carbonate Chemistry (Slides 45–46): Dissolved $CO_2$ reacts with seawater to form carbonic acid: $CO_2 + H_2O \leftrightarrow H_2CO_3 \leftrightarrow H^+ + HCO_3^-$. The surplus hydrogen ions react with carbonate ions: $H^+ + CO_3^{2-} \leftrightarrow HCO_3^-$. This depletes available carbonate ions ($CO_3^{2-}$), depressing the calcium carbonate saturation state ($\Omega < 1$) and causing the shells of pteropods, corals, and shellfish to dissolve.

2. Enhanced Coal-Bed Methane (ECBM) Leakage Risk (Slides 48–49): Injecting $CO_2$ into unmineable coal seams displaces adsorbed methane ($CH_4$) because coal affinity for $CO_2$ is ~twice that of $CH_4$. However, methane has a Global Warming Potential 72 times that of $CO_2$ over a 20-year horizon ($GWP_{20} = 72$). If even a tiny fraction ($>1.5\%$) of displaced methane escapes through fractured caprock, the net climate benefit is completely wiped out.

3. Novacem Carbon-Negative Cement (Slide 51): Traditional Portland cement manufacturing produces ~5% of global anthropogenic $CO_2$ emissions (calcination of limestone: $CaCO_3 \to CaO + CO_2$). Novacem, developed at Imperial College London, uses magnesium silicates ($Mg_3Si_2O_5(OH)_4$) that require lower kiln temperatures and actively absorb atmospheric $CO_2$ during curing, resulting in a net carbon-negative cement absorbing ~0.5 tonnes $CO_2$ per tonne produced.

Governing Quantitative Model
$$\Omega = \frac{[Ca^{2+}][CO_3^{2-}]}{K'_{sp}} < 1 \implies \text{Shell Dissolution} \quad | \quad GWP_{20}(CH_4) = 72 \times CO_2$$
⚠️
Midsem/Endsem Exam Checkpoint for These Slides:

Question (Click / Space to Flip)
What is the Additionality Principle in CDM projects?
🔄 Click card to reveal answer
Answer
The requirement that a project's greenhouse gas reductions must be additional to any that would occur in the absence of the certified CDM project activity (i.e., not business-as-usual).
Question (Click / Space to Flip)
What are High-Nutrient, Low-Chlorophyll (HNLC) ocean regions?
🔄 Click card to reveal answer
Answer
Ocean areas (such as the Southern Ocean and subarctic Pacific) with abundant nitrate and phosphate but low phytoplankton productivity due to the lack of the limiting trace micronutrient Iron ($Fe$).
Question (Click / Space to Flip)
Why does liquid $CO_2$ sink only below ~3,000 meters in the ocean?
🔄 Click card to reveal answer
Answer
Because liquid $CO_2$ is more compressible than seawater. Above ~3,000 m it is buoyant and rises; below ~3,000 m (pressure $> 30\,\text{MPa}$), its density exceeds that of seawater, allowing it to sink.
High-Yield Insights

Exam Traps, Fallacies & Professor Emphasis Points

Common student misconceptions and subtle distinctions tested in Midsem and Endsem exams.

  • ⚠️ Trap: Confusing CDM with Joint Implementation (JI). CDM involves developed Annex I nations investing in non-Annex I developing nations. Joint Implementation involves developed nations investing in other Annex I nations (typically economies in transition).
  • ⚠️ Exam Tip: Be ready to write out the carbonate equilibrium equations showing that adding $CO_2$ decreases free carbonate ion $[CO_3^{2-}]$ concentration, harming calcifying marine organisms.

Question 2
What is the primary ecological risk associated with large-scale ocean iron fertilization?
A Immediate freezing of the ocean surface
B Deep-water oxygen depletion (hypoxia) during organic matter decay and potential release of powerful $N_2O$ gas
C Massive loss of oceanic salinity
D Accelerated solar ultraviolet penetration
Explanation: As massive stimulated blooms sink, heterotrophic bacteria consume dissolved oxygen to decompose the biomass, producing anoxic dead zones and generating nitrous oxide ($N_2O$, GWP=273).
