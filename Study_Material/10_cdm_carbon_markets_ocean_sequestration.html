Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) & Ocean Sequestration

Kyoto Flexible Instruments, Additionality Rubrics, and Oceanic Carbon Injection
💡 Why This Lecture Matters: The Clean Development Mechanism (Article 12 of the Kyoto Protocol) pioneered international market-based carbon finance, enabling Annex I developed nations to invest in emission-reduction projects in developing nations in exchange for Certified Emission Reductions (CERs). Concurrently, the ocean—Earth's largest active carbon pool—has been investigated for ocean iron fertilization and direct liquid $CO_2$ injection, raising complex ecological questions regarding ocean acidification and marine food web collapse.

What is the Additionality Principle in CDM projects?
🔄 Click card to reveal answer
Answer
The requirement that a project's greenhouse gas reductions must be additional to any that would occur in the absence of the certified CDM project activity (i.e., not business-as-usual).
Question (Click / Space to Flip)
What are High-Nutrient, Low-Chlorophyll (HNLC) ocean regions?
🔄 Click card to reveal answer
Answer
Ocean areas (such as the Southern Ocean and subarctic Pacific) with abundant nitrate and phosphate but low phytoplankton productivity due to the lack of the limiting trace micronutrient Iron ($Fe$).
Question (Click / Space to Flip)
Why does liquid $CO_2$ sink only below ~3,000 meters in the ocean?
🔄 Click card to reveal answer
Answer
Because liquid $CO_2$ is more compressible than seawater. Above ~3,000 m it is buoyant and rises; below ~3,000 m (pressure $> 30\,\text{MPa}$), its density exceeds that of seawater, allowing it to sink.

Exam Traps, Fallacies & Professor Emphasis Points

Common student misconceptions and subtle distinctions tested in Midsem and Endsem exams.

  • ⚠️ Trap: Confusing CDM with Joint Implementation (JI). CDM involves developed Annex I nations investing in non-Annex I developing nations. Joint Implementation involves developed nations investing in other Annex I nations (typically economies in transition).
