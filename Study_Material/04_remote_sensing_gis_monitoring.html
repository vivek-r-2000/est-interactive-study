Remote Sensing & GIS in Environmental Technology

Electromagnetic Spectral Signatures, Earth Observation Satellites, and Spatial Analysis
💡 Why This Lecture Matters: Ground-based environmental sampling is spatially sparse and logistically expensive. Remote Sensing (RS) and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) provide synoptic, multi-temporal, and multi-spectral observation of the Earth's surface. From tracking deforestation in the Western Ghats to mapping Urban Heat Islands (UHI) and water turbidity, satellite remote sensing is the primary quantitative monitoring tool of modern environmental engineering.

Remote sensing

Deep Dive

Topic Synthesis: Remote Sensing Principles, EMS Bands & Satellite Sensors

1. Physics of Remote Sensing (Slides 1–3): Remote sensing is the acquisition of physical data about Earth's surface without making direct physical contact, by measuring reflected solar radiance and emitted terrestrial thermal radiation across specific bands of the Electromagnetic Spectrum (EMS).

2. EMS Partitioning & Atmospheric Windows:

  • Visible (0.4–0.7 $\mu m$): Blue (0.45–0.51 $\mu m$), Green (0.53–0.59 $\mu m$), Red (0.64–0.67 $\mu m$). Healthy chlorophyll absorbs Blue and Red strongly for photosynthesis while reflecting Green.
  • Near-Infrared (NIR, 0.7–1.3 $\mu m$): Uniquely sensitive to plant leaf internal mesophyll cell structure; healthy vegetation displays intense high reflectance (the 'NIR plateau').
  • Shortwave-Infrared (SWIR, 1.3–2.5 $\mu m$): Strongly absorbed by plant leaf water content and soil moisture; critical for drought stress detection.
  • Thermal-Infrared (TIR, 8–14 $\mu m$): Measures blackbody thermal emission for Land Surface Temperature (LST).

3. Satellite Sensors (Slide 5): Landsat series (MSS, TM, ETM+, OLI), ISRO IRS/LISS sensors, and ESA Sentinel-2 multispectral instruments providing spatial, spectral, temporal, and radiometric resolutions.

Governing Quantitative Model
$$NDVI = \frac{NIR - \text{Red}}{NIR + \text{Red}} \quad | \quad \text{Wien's Displacement Law: } \lambda_{max} = \frac{2898}{T}\,\mu m\cdot K$$
⚠️
Midsem/Endsem Exam Checkpoint for These Slides:

Why do healthy plants have high NIR and low Red reflectance? Be prepared to sketch the classic vegetation spectral reflectance curve and write the NDVI formula.

FOREST TYPE

Deep Dive

Topic Synthesis: Mangrove Mapping, 1976–1999 Change Detection & Katchal Island

1. Mangrove Forest Type Mapping (Slide 6): Mangroves represent critical coastal buffer zones with unique spectral signatures. Remote sensing discriminates dense mangrove stands from mudflats and inland forests using False Color Composites (FCC: NIR in red channel).

2. Multi-Decadal Change Detection (Slide 7): Comparative analysis between Landsat MSS (1976, 80m resolution) and IRS LISS III (1999, 23.5m resolution) in a sensitive coastal study region revealed an overall forest loss of 12.5% (5.0% interior forest fragmentation, 7.5% coastal mangrove clearing), with a corresponding rise in degraded scrub and agricultural land.

3. Katchal Island Post-Tsunami Remote Sensing (Slide 9): The December 26, 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami devastated Katchal Island in the Nicobar archipelago. Multi-temporal satellite imagery tracked co-seismic land subsidence, tidal wave scouring of entire coastal forests, and altered coastline geomorphology.

Governing Quantitative Model
$$\Delta \text{Area} = \text{Area}_{t_2} - \text{Area}_{t_1} \quad | \quad \text{Overall Accuracy} = \frac{\sum \text{Correct Pixels}}{\text{Total Pixels}} \times 100\%$$
⚠️
Midsem/Endsem Exam Checkpoint for These Slides:

Know the sensors compared in the instructor's case study: 1976 Landsat MSS (80m) vs 1999 IRS LISS III (23.5m), and the exact 12.5% forest loss statistic.

💡 What This Slide Teaches:

Assessing the water quality dynamics

Deep Dive

Topic Synthesis: Kollam Coastal Water Quality, Nilambur AHP Landslide Mapping & GHMC LST

1. Coastal Water Quality Dynamics in Kollam, Kerala (Slide 12): High-resolution Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery mapped Total Suspended Solids (TSS), turbidity, and chlorophyll-a concentrations in the Arabian Sea off the coast of Kollam, tracking industrial effluent plumes and monsoonal sediment discharge.

2. Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) for Landslides in Nilambur (Slide 13): In Nilambur Taluk, Kerala (Western Ghats), catastrophic monsoon debris flows occur on steep slopes. AHP multi-criteria decision evaluation weighs diverse spatial thematic layers: slope angle, elevation, lithology, soil depth, distance to fault lines, and LULC. Pairwise comparison matrices yield a Landslide Susceptibility Index (LSI) with a validated Consistency Ratio ($CR < 0.10$).

3. Urban Heat Islands & Green Space Mitigation in Hyderabad (Slide 14): Study within Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) using Landsat-8 TIRS. Quantified how urban parks, water bodies, and tree canopy cool Land Surface Temperature (LST) by $2^\circ\text{C}$ to $5^\circ\text{C}$ relative to dense asphalt and concrete built-up areas via evapotranspirative cooling.

Governing Quantitative Model
$$LSI = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \cdot r_i \quad | \quad CR = \frac{CI}{RI} < 0.10 \quad | \quad LST = \frac{T_B}{1 + (\lambda T_B / \rho) \ln \varepsilon}$$
⚠️
Midsem/Endsem Exam Checkpoint for These Slides:

AHP Landslide Susceptibility Mapping is a favorite exam topic. Be prepared to explain pairwise comparison matrices, the role of Consistency Ratio ($CR < 0.1$), and how urban green spaces mitigate LST.

Slide 15: Modeling Forest Dynamics, Fragmentation, and Carbon

📝 Slide Text & Key Bullets
•Sequestration in Idukki District, Kerala, India
💡 What This Slide Teaches:

Modeling Forest Dynamics, Fragmentation, and Carbon

Deep Dive

Topic Synthesis: Idukki Forest Carbon, Mining in Manuguru, MaxEnt Aphids & Kondapalli

1. Forest Dynamics & Carbon in Idukki, Kerala (Slide 15): Combined multi-sensor satellite imagery with field biometric plots in Idukki District. Applied allometric regression equations to convert canopy height and Crown Closure into Aboveground Biomass (AGB) and carbon stocks ($tC/ha$), modeling carbon sequestration losses under agricultural encroachment.

2. Manuguru Coal Belt Mining Pressure, Telangana (Slide 16): Multi-decadal remote sensing of the Singareni Collieries opencast coal belt in Manuguru. Quantified large-scale deforestation, topsoil stripping, overburden dump growth, and calculated the net loss in total ecosystem service valuation (ESV).

3. MaxEnt Ecological Niche Modeling of Pea Aphid (Slide 17): Maximum Entropy (MaxEnt) machine learning algorithm used bioclimatic variables (WorldClim temperature and precipitation surfaces) to predict the geographic range shift of the agricultural pest Pea Aphid (*Acyrthosiphon pisum*) under IPCC future warming scenarios.

4. Kondapalli Reserve Forest Patch Fragmentation (Slide 18): Landscape metrics (Mean Patch Size, Perimeter-Area Ratio, Euclidean Nearest-Neighbor Distance) quantified remnant patch isolation and edge effects in Andhra Pradesh.

Governing Quantitative Model
$$AGB = a \cdot (DBH)^b \cdot H^c \quad | \quad P(x) = \frac{1}{Z} \exp\left(\sum_{i} \lambda_i f_i(x)\right) \quad \text{(MaxEnt Probability)}$$
⚠️
Midsem/Endsem Exam Checkpoint for These Slides:

Understand how MaxEnt uses presence-only occurrence records with environmental raster layers to model species distribution shifts under climate change.

Active Recall Flashcards

Test yourself on essential slide definitions, reactions, and principles before flipping.

Question (Click / Space to Flip)
Why does healthy vegetation appear bright red in a Standard False Color Composite (FCC)?
🔄 Click card to reveal answer
Answer
Because in standard FCC, the sensor's Near-Infrared (NIR) band is assigned to the monitor's Red display channel. Healthy leaves reflect 40-50% of NIR radiation due to spongy mesophyll scattering, producing intense red coloration.
Question (Click / Space to Flip)
What is the physical significance of the 'Red Edge' in vegetation spectroscopy?
🔄 Click card to reveal answer
Answer
The sharp transition between strong chlorophyll absorption in the red band (~0.67 $\mu$m) and intense cellular scattering in the NIR (~0.70-0.75 $\mu$m). Its steepness correlates with leaf chlorophyll content and plant health.
Question (Click / Space to Flip)
What is the formula for Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI)?
🔄 Click card to reveal answer
Answer
$NDWI = \frac{\text{Green} - \text{NIR}}{\text{Green} + \text{NIR}}$. It enhances open water bodies while suppressing soil and vegetation features.
High-Yield Insights

Exam Traps, Fallacies & Professor Emphasis Points

Common student misconceptions and subtle distinctions tested in Midsem and Endsem exams.

  • ⚠️ Trap: Confusing Spatial Resolution with Temporal Resolution. High spatial resolution (e.g. 0.5m WorldView) often comes with poor temporal revisit (weeks), whereas MODIS offers daily revisit at coarse 250m-1km spatial resolution.
  • ⚠️ Exam Trap: Thinking water has high NDVI. Water absorbs almost 100% of NIR radiation, making $\rho_{\text{NIR}} \approx 0$, which results in a distinctly negative NDVI.
Knowledge Verification

Concept Check Quiz

Instant feedback on core conceptual and quantitative questions from the lecture slides.

Question 1
A pixel in a satellite image has a Red reflectance $\rho_{\text{Red}} = 0.05$ and a Near-Infrared reflectance $\rho_{\text{NIR}} = 0.45$. What is its NDVI, and what land cover does it represent?
A NDVI = 0.80, representing dense healthy vegetation canopy
B NDVI = 0.50, representing dry sandy soil
C NDVI = -0.80, representing an ocean body
D NDVI = 0.00, representing cloud shadow
Explanation: $NDVI = (0.45 - 0.05) / (0.45 + 0.05) = 0.40 / 0.50 = 0.80$. Values above 0.7 signify dense, active photosynthesizing vegetation.
Question 2
Which atmospheric window is exploited by satellite thermal sensors to measure Sea Surface Temperature (SST) and Land Surface Temperature (LST)?
A 0.4 to 0.7 $\mu$m (Visible)
B 8.0 to 12.0 $\mu$m (Thermal Infrared)
C 1.4 to 1.9 $\mu$m (Water vapor absorption band)
D 0.1 to 0.3 $\mu$m (Ultraviolet)
Explanation: The 8-12 $\mu$m thermal infrared atmospheric window coincides with Earth's peak thermal emission and experiences minimal atmospheric attenuation.
