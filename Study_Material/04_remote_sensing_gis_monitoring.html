Remote Sensing & GIS in Environmental Technology

Electromagnetic Spectral Signatures, Earth Observation Satellites, and Spatial Analysis
💡 Why This Topic Matters:
Ground-based environmental sampling is spatially sparse and logistically expensive. Remote Sensing (RS) and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) provide synoptic, multi-temporal, and multi-spectral observation of the Earth's surface. From tracking deforestation in the Western Ghats to mapping Urban Heat Islands (UHI) and water turbidity, satellite remote sensing is the primary quantitative monitoring tool of modern environmental engineering.
Physics Spectral Reflectance Curves

Healthy vegetation displays a distinctive 'Red Edge': strong absorption in Blue & Red (chlorophyll pigments) and high reflectance in Near-Infrared (spongy mesophyll cellular scattering). Water absorbs strongly in NIR, while dry soil shows monotonic reflectance increase.

Sensors Sensor Resolutions

1. Spatial (pixel size, e.g. Sentinel-2: 10m, Landsat-8: 30m). 2. Spectral (band width & count). 3. Radiometric (bit depth, 12-bit / 16-bit). 4. Temporal (revisit cycle, Sentinel-2: 5 days).

Analytics Vegetation & Water Indices

Normalized Difference Vegetation Index ($NDVI = \frac{NIR - Red}{NIR + Red}$) normalizes illumination variations and correlates directly with photosynthetic biomass and leaf area index (LAI).

Core Mechanics

Electromagnetic Radiation & Spectral Signature Mathematics

Planck's law and Wien's Displacement Law govern electromagnetic emissions from environmental surfaces:

$$\lambda_{\max} = \frac{b}{T} \quad (b = 2897.8\,\mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K})$$

For the Sun ($T \approx 5800\,\text{K}$), $\lambda_{\max} \approx 0.5\,\mu\text{m}$ (visible green-blue). For Earth ($T \approx 288\,\text{K}$), $\lambda_{\max} \approx 9.7\,\mu\text{m}$ (thermal infrared).

The False Color Composite (FCC) standard places Near-Infrared (NIR) into the Red display channel, Red into Green, and Green into Blue. Healthy dense vegetation consequently appears bright crimson red in standard FCC imagery.

Figure 1

Remote Sensing Electromagnetic Interaction & Sensor Geometry

📍 Class-4-RS-EVS-upload.pdf, Slide 2
🔍 Click diagram to zoom in full lightbox
👁️ What the Diagram Shows: A comprehensive diagram tracing solar radiant energy path: transmission through atmospheric windows, surface reflection/absorption, and detection by satellite sensors.
🔬 Scientific & Environmental Mechanism: Solar radiation travels through the atmosphere where it encounters Rayleigh scattering (by air molecules, proportional to $\lambda^{-4}$), Mie scattering (by aerosols), and selective gaseous absorption ($O_3, CO_2, H_2O$). Only radiation passing through atmospheric windows reaches the ground and reflects back to the satellite detector.
📊 Quantitative / Mathematical Model: Radiance received at sensor: $L_{\text{sensor}} = \frac{1}{\pi} \rho \tau_v (E_0 \tau_s \cos \theta_s + E_{\text{down}}) + L_p$, where $\rho$ is surface reflectance, $\tau$ is atmospheric transmittance, and $L_p$ is atmospheric path radiance.
⚠️ Exam Trap: Forgetting that Rayleigh scattering dominates the blue spectrum (giving sky its blue color and causing haze in satellite blue bands), while NIR radiation penetrates haze far more effectively.
Figure 2

Spectral Reflectance Curves for Pure Environmental Endmembers

📍 Class-4-RS-EVS-upload.pdf, Slide 6
🔍 Click diagram to zoom in full lightbox
👁️ What the Diagram Shows: Curves comparing the percentage reflectance across visible, near-infrared (NIR), and shortwave infrared (SWIR) wavelengths for healthy vegetation, bare dry soil, and clear water.
🔬 Scientific & Environmental Mechanism: 1. Vegetation: Low visible reflectance due to chlorophyll a/b absorption, sharp rise at 0.7 $\mu$m (the Red Edge) to ~50% NIR reflectance from cellular scattering, and water absorption dips at 1.4 and 1.9 $\mu$m. 2. Water: High absorption in NIR (reflectance near zero). 3. Soil: Smooth monotonic increase from visible into SWIR.
📊 Quantitative / Mathematical Model: The basis of NDVI: $NDVI = \frac{\rho_{\text{NIR}} - \rho_{\text{Red}}}{\rho_{\text{NIR}} + \rho_{\text{Red}}}$. Because vegetation maximizes the difference between high NIR and low Red, NDVI is maximized (+0.7 to +0.9).
⚠️ Exam Trap: Turbid or sediment-laden water reflects visible light (green/red) much more than clear water, but still drops to nearly zero in the NIR spectrum.
Rigorous Quantitative Model

Key Remote Sensing Indices

$$NDVI = \frac{\rho_{\text{NIR}} - \rho_{\text{Red}}}{\rho_{\text{NIR}} + \rho_{\text{Red}}}, \quad NDWI = \frac{\rho_{\text{Green}} - \rho_{\text{NIR}}}{\rho_{\text{Green}} + \rho_{\text{NIR}}}$$

NDVI ranges from $-1.0$ to $+1.0$. Water bodies yield negative values ($-0.5$ to $0$), bare soil yields $0.1$ to $0.2$, and dense healthy forest canopies reach $0.7$ to $0.9$.

Active Recall Flashcards

Test yourself on essential definitions, reactions, and principles before flipping.

Question (Click / Space to Flip)
Why does healthy vegetation appear bright red in a Standard False Color Composite (FCC)?
🔄 Click card to reveal answer
Answer
Because in standard FCC, the sensor's Near-Infrared (NIR) band is assigned to the monitor's Red display channel. Healthy leaves reflect 40-50% of NIR radiation due to spongy mesophyll scattering, producing intense red coloration.
Question (Click / Space to Flip)
What is the physical significance of the 'Red Edge' in vegetation spectroscopy?
🔄 Click card to reveal answer
Answer
The sharp transition between strong chlorophyll absorption in the red band (~0.67 $\mu$m) and intense cellular scattering in the NIR (~0.70-0.75 $\mu$m). Its steepness correlates with leaf chlorophyll content and plant health.
Question (Click / Space to Flip)
What is the formula for Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI)?
🔄 Click card to reveal answer
Answer
$NDWI = \frac{\text{Green} - \text{NIR}}{\text{Green} + \text{NIR}}$. It enhances open water bodies while suppressing soil and vegetation features.
High-Yield Insights

Exam Traps, Fallacies & Professor Emphasis Points

Common student misconceptions and subtle distinctions tested in Midsem and Endsem exams.

  • ⚠️ Trap: Confusing Spatial Resolution with Temporal Resolution. High spatial resolution (e.g. 0.5m WorldView) often comes with poor temporal revisit (weeks), whereas MODIS offers daily revisit at coarse 250m-1km spatial resolution.
  • ⚠️ Exam Trap: Thinking water has high NDVI. Water absorbs almost 100% of NIR radiation, making $\rho_{\text{NIR}} \approx 0$, which results in a distinctly negative NDVI.
Knowledge Verification

Concept Check Quiz

Instant feedback on core conceptual and quantitative questions.

Question 1
A pixel in a satellite image has a Red reflectance $\rho_{\text{Red}} = 0.05$ and a Near-Infrared reflectance $\rho_{\text{NIR}} = 0.45$. What is its NDVI, and what land cover does it represent?
A NDVI = 0.80, representing dense healthy vegetation canopy
B NDVI = 0.50, representing dry sandy soil
C NDVI = -0.80, representing an ocean body
D NDVI = 0.00, representing cloud shadow
Explanation: $NDVI = (0.45 - 0.05) / (0.45 + 0.05) = 0.40 / 0.50 = 0.80$. Values above 0.7 signify dense, active photosynthesizing vegetation.
Question 2
Which atmospheric window is exploited by satellite thermal sensors to measure Sea Surface Temperature (SST) and Land Surface Temperature (LST)?
A 0.4 to 0.7 $\mu$m (Visible)
B 8.0 to 12.0 $\mu$m (Thermal Infrared)
C 1.4 to 1.9 $\mu$m (Water vapor absorption band)
D 0.1 to 0.3 $\mu$m (Ultraviolet)
Explanation: The 8-12 $\mu$m thermal infrared atmospheric window coincides with Earth's peak thermal emission and experiences minimal atmospheric attenuation.
