Remote Sensing & GIS in Environmental Technology

Electromagnetic Spectral Signatures, Earth Observation Satellites, and Spatial Analysis
Core objective: Ground-based environmental sampling is spatially sparse and logistically expensive. Remote Sensing (RS) and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) provide synoptic, multi-temporal, and multi-spectral observation of the Earth's surface. From tracking deforestation in the Western Ghats to mapping Urban Heat Islands (UHI) and water turbidity, satellite remote sensing is the primary quantitative monitoring tool of modern environmental engineering.

Active recall flashcards

Review definitions, reactions, and governing laws from the lecture slides before flipping.

Question (click to flip)
Why does healthy vegetation appear bright red in a Standard False Color Composite (FCC)?
Click card to reveal answer
Answer
Because in standard FCC, the sensor's Near-Infrared (NIR) band is assigned to the monitor's Red display channel. Healthy leaves reflect 40-50% of NIR radiation due to spongy mesophyll scattering, producing intense red coloration.
Question (click to flip)
What is the physical significance of the 'Red Edge' in vegetation spectroscopy?
Click card to reveal answer
Answer
The sharp transition between strong chlorophyll absorption in the red band (~0.67 $\mu$m) and intense cellular scattering in the NIR (~0.70-0.75 $\mu$m). Its steepness correlates with leaf chlorophyll content and plant health.
Question (click to flip)
What is the formula for Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI)?
Click card to reveal answer
Answer
$NDWI = \frac{\text{Green} - \text{NIR}}{\text{Green} + \text{NIR}}$. It enhances open water bodies while suppressing soil and vegetation features.
Exam checkpoints

Exam traps and common pitfalls

Common student misconceptions and subtle distinctions tested in exams.

  • Exam trap: Confusing Spatial Resolution with Temporal Resolution. High spatial resolution (e.g. 0.5m WorldView) often comes with poor temporal revisit (weeks), whereas MODIS offers daily revisit at coarse 250m-1km spatial resolution.
  • Exam trap: Thinking water has high NDVI. Water absorbs almost 100% of NIR radiation, making $\rho_{\text{NIR}} \approx 0$, which results in a distinctly negative NDVI.
Self evaluation

Concept check quiz

Verify your understanding of core concepts from this lecture deck.

Question 1
A pixel in a satellite image has a Red reflectance $\rho_{\text{Red}} = 0.05$ and a Near-Infrared reflectance $\rho_{\text{NIR}} = 0.45$. What is its NDVI, and what land cover does it represent?
A NDVI = 0.80, representing dense healthy vegetation canopy
B NDVI = 0.50, representing dry sandy soil
C NDVI = -0.80, representing an ocean body
D NDVI = 0.00, representing cloud shadow
Explanation: $NDVI = (0.45 - 0.05) / (0.45 + 0.05) = 0.40 / 0.50 = 0.80$. Values above 0.7 signify dense, active photosynthesizing vegetation.
Question 2
Which atmospheric window is exploited by satellite thermal sensors to measure Sea Surface Temperature (SST) and Land Surface Temperature (LST)?
A 0.4 to 0.7 $\mu$m (Visible)
B 8.0 to 12.0 $\mu$m (Thermal Infrared)
C 1.4 to 1.9 $\mu$m (Water vapor absorption band)
D 0.1 to 0.3 $\mu$m (Ultraviolet)
Explanation: The 8-12 $\mu$m thermal infrared atmospheric window coincides with Earth's peak thermal emission and experiences minimal atmospheric attenuation.
