### B. What is / Definitions
1. **Tipping Point:**
   - **Sources:** `EST_mid1_2024.pdf` (Section II, Q4)
   - **Answer:** A critical environmental threshold where a minor additional perturbation triggers a self-reinforcing, non-linear, and often irreversible regime shift in the Earth system (e.g., Arctic summer sea-ice loss, West Antarctic Ice Sheet collapse, Amazon rainforest dieback).
2. **Gray Goo:**
   - **Sources:** `EST_mid1_2024.pdf` (Section II, Q5), `EVS_papers_2019.pdf` (Mid 2019 Part II, Q1a)
   - **Answer:** A catastrophic existential risk scenario in molecular nanotechnology (introduced by Eric Drexler) where self-replicating nanobots multiply out of control, consuming all planetary carbon and biomass to replicate themselves.
3. **Global Terrestrial Stilling:**
   - **Sources:** `EST_mid1_2024.pdf` (Section II, Q7)
   - **Answer:** A documented multi-decadal decrease in near-surface terrestrial wind speeds globally (average decline of ~0.3 m/s per decade), driven by increasing surface roughness (forest regrowth, urbanization) and weakened polar-equatorial temperature gradients reducing atmospheric circulation intensity.
4. **Black Swan Theory:**
   - **Sources:** `EVS_papers_2019.pdf` (Mid 2019 Part II, Q1b)
   - **Answer:** Formulated by Nassim Nicholas Taleb: an event defined by three attributes: (1) Extreme rarity/unpredictability (an outlier), (2) Massive, transformative, or systemic impact, and (3) Retrospective explainability (humans rationalize it after it occurs). Climate tipping cascades represent catastrophic environmental Black Swans.
5. **Nuclear Winter:**
   - **Sources:** `EST_mid1_2024.pdf` (Section II, Q8), `EST_mid1_2022.pdf` (Q9), `EVS_papers_2019.pdf` (Mid 2019 Part II)
   - **Answer:** The severe, prolonged global cooling effect caused by widespread fires following nuclear warfare. Hundreds of millions of tons of black, sooty carbon smoke are lofted into the stratosphere, blocking incoming solar radiation for weeks to months, plummeting continental temperatures below freezing, halting photosynthesis, and destroying the ozone layer, inducing global agricultural collapse and famine.

### C. Conceptual Explanations & Mechanisms
1. **Volcanic Eruptions from Glacier Melt:**
   - **Question:** *"Why volcanic eruption become more frequent when glaciers melt?"*
   - **Sources:** `EST_mid1_2024.pdf` (Section I, Q3), `EVS_mid2_2018.pdf` (Q6a)
   - **Answer:** Massive glaciers exert colossal lithospheric pressure on the underlying crust. As glaciers retreat and melt due to global warming, this weight is removed (isostatic rebound / depressurization). Depressurization lowers the melting point of rock in the mantle and allows magma chambers to expand and gas bubbles to nucleate, drastically increasing explosive volcanic eruption frequencies.
2. **Hypercanes vs. Present-Day Hurricanes & Ozone Layer Impact:**
   - **Questions:**
     - *"How Hypercane differs from present day hurricane?"* (`EST_mid1_2024.pdf` Section I, Q4)
     - *"How hypercane impacts Earth's Ozone layer?"* (`EVS_end_2023.pdf` Section I, Q3)
   - **Answer:**
     - **Difference:** Standard tropical cyclones require water temperatures $\ge 26.5^\circ\text{C}$ and max out around 300–320 km/h with vertical extents in the troposphere (~15 km). Hypercanes require ocean temperatures reaching $\sim 50^\circ\text{C}$ (e.g., triggered by large asteroid impacts or massive volcanic/climate feedback), generating supersonic/near-extreme wind speeds ($>800\text{ km/h}$) and central pressures $<650\text{ hPa}$.
     - **Ozone Impact:** Hypercanes possess massive vertical columns reaching up to 40–50 km directly into the upper stratosphere. They loft massive volumes of water vapor, ocean sea-salt aerosols, and reactive chlorine radicals into the stratosphere, which catalytically break down stratospheric ozone ($O_3$), destroying Earth's UV-protective shield.
3. **Ocean Darkening:**
   - **Question:** *"With respect to climate change, why oceans become dark?"*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_mid2_2018.pdf` (Q6b)
   - **Answer:** Climate warming intensifies extreme precipitation and rapid permafrost/glacier thawing. This runoff flushes massive loads of dissolved organic matter (DOM), humic substances, and eroded silt/sediments from land into coastal marine waters, dramatically reducing light transmission and altering coastal photic zones.
4. **Increased Lightning Events:**
   - **Question:** *"Why there will be more lightning process in the event of climate change?"*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_end_2022.pdf` (Section IX, Q1a)
   - **Answer:** Higher ambient temperatures increase evaporation and atmospheric water vapor capacity (by $\sim 7\%$ per $1^\circ\text{C}$ warming via the Clausius-Clapeyron relation). This elevates convective available potential energy (CAPE), generating taller, more energetic convective thunderclouds where ice crystal and graupel collisions build large electrical charge separations, increasing lightning strikes.
5. **Arctic Summer Ice Loss as the "Single Greatest Threat":**
   - **Question:** *"Why melting of Arctic summer ice is considered to be the single greatest threat?"*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_end_2023.pdf` (Section II, Q19)
   - **Answer:** Melting bright white sea ice (albedo $\sim 0.6–0.85$) exposes dark open ocean water (albedo $\sim 0.06$). The ocean absorbs over $90\%$ of incoming solar radiation instead of reflecting it, driving the **ice-albedo positive feedback loop**. This polar amplification destabilizes the northern jet stream, rapidly accelerates the circumpolar permafrost thaw (threatening to release hundreds of gigatons of methane clathrates), and destabilizes the Greenland ice sheet.

### B. Core Concepts & Comparisons
1. **Mature Forest Carbon Saturation:**
   - **Question:** *"In mature forests all of the gross primary productivity is either used up in _______ or returned to soil as ___________ with no net addition to the standing biomass." / "In natural forests, there is a net addition to standing biomass leading to carbon storage only until maturity - why not later?"*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_quiz3_2020.pdf` (Q1b), `EVS_mid2_2018.pdf` (Q1b), `EVS_end_2019.pdf` (Part II, Q6)
   - **Answer:** 
     - Blanks: **plant/autotrophic respiration**; **litterfall / dead organic matter (debris)**.
     - Reason: In juvenile/growing forests, Net Primary Production (NPP) substantially exceeds heterotrophic respiration, accumulating net carbon in woody trunks. As a forest reaches ecological climax/maturity, the total living tissue maintenance respiration increases, while tree mortality and litterfall decomposition by soil microbes match annual photosynthetic assimilation ($GPP \approx R_{\text{auto}} + R_{\text{hetero}}$), resulting in net-zero biomass accumulation.
2. **Afforestation vs. Reforestation:**
   - **Question:** *"Differentiate between afforestation and reforestation."*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_quiz3_2020.pdf` (Q3a), `EVS_end_2019.pdf` (Part II, Q4), `EVS_quiz4_2020.pdf` (Q7, Q20)
   - **Answer:**
     - **Afforestation:** Establishing a new forest by planting seeds/trees on land that historically has **not** contained forest cover for a prolonged period (typically $>50\text{ years}$ under UNFCCC definitions, such as arid lands or degraded pastures).
     - **Reforestation:** Re-establishing forest cover through planting or natural regeneration on land that **previously supported forests** but was converted or cleared due to deforestation, logging, or fire.
3. **Agroforestry for Carbon Mitigation:**
   - **Question:** *"What is Agroforestry? Why is agroforestry an attractive option for carbon mitigation?"*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_quiz3_2020.pdf` (Q5), `EVS_mid2_2018.pdf` (Q3d), `EVS_end_2019.pdf` (Part II, Q5)
   - **Answer:**
     - **Definition:** An integrated land-use management system that intentionally combines woody perennials (trees/shrubs) with agricultural crops and/or livestock on the same land-management unit.
     - **Mitigation Advantages:**
       1. **High Sequestration Rate:** Deep tree root systems sequester carbon deep into the subsoil profile where it resists rapid oxidation.
       2. **No Conflict with Food Security:** Farmers produce timber/fruits without taking fertile land completely out of food production.
       3. **Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) Enhancement:** Continuous leaf litter and nitrogen fixation enhance microbial biomass and reduce synthetic fertilizer demands (lowering $N_2O$ emissions).
4. **Riparian Forest Buffers and Functions:**
   - **Question:** *"What are riparian forest buffers and their functions?"*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_end_2023.pdf` (Section II, Q18), `EVS_end_2019.pdf` (Part II, Q3)
   - **Answer:** Vegetated forested strips adjacent to streams, rivers, ponds, and wetlands.
     - **Functions:** (1) Intercept agricultural runoff, trapping up to 90% of sediments, phosphorus, and excess nitrates before reaching aquatic ecosystems; (2) Deep roots stabilize stream banks and prevent erosion; (3) Provide shade to lower stream water temperatures for cold-water aquatic species; (4) High-density carbon sequestration in both woody biomass and saturated hydric soils.
5. **Conservation Tillage:**
   - **Question:** *"What is conservation agricultural tillage and its uses?"*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_end_2023.pdf` (Section II, Q16), `EVS_mid2_2018.pdf` (Q4b)
   - **Answer:** An agricultural practice (including zero-tillage or minimum-tillage) that leaves at least 30% of the soil surface covered with crop residue after planting.
     - **Uses:** Minimizes soil mechanical disturbance, prevents oxidation of soil organic matter, preserves soil microbial mycorrhizal networks, conserves soil moisture, and curbs wind/water erosion.
6. **Remote Sensing in Environmental Studies:**
   - **Questions:**
     - *"How vegetation and deeper water bodies appear in infrared composite satellite data?"*
     - *"List any six applications of remote sensing technology in environmental studies."*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_end_2019.pdf` (Part I, Q1–3), `EVS_mid2_2018.pdf` (Q3b, Q10c)
   - **Answer:**
     - **Infrared Appearance:** Healthy green vegetation strongly reflects Near-Infrared (NIR) radiation while absorbing red light (chlorophyll absorption), appearing as **bright vibrant red** in False Color Composite (FCC) satellite imagery. Deep, clear water bodies absorb almost all NIR radiation and appear **dark black or dark navy blue**.
     - **6 Applications:** (1) Forest canopy and deforestation monitoring (e.g. Landsat grid), (2) Desertification tracking, (3) Glacial retreat and ice shelf breakup measurement, (4) Wetland mapping, (5) Agricultural crop yield and drought monitoring (NDVI), (6) Urban heat island mapping.
7. **International Carbon Sequestration Projects:**
   - **Question:** *"Describe any one international carbon sequestration project."*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_quiz3_2020.pdf` (Q6), `EVS_end_2019.pdf` (Part II, Q7)
   - **Answer:**
     - *Option A — **INFAPRO (Innoprise-FACE Foundation Rainforest Rehabilitation Project, Sabah, Malaysia):** Collaborated to rehabilitate degraded Dipterocarp rainforests by enrichment planting of indigenous tree species and liberation thinning, sequestering millions of tonnes of $CO_2$ over a 60-year concession.
     - *Option B — **Plan Vivo / Scolel'te Project (Chiapas, Mexico):** A community-based carbon management initiative where indigenous Mayan smallholders receive payments for ecosystem services (carbon credits) by establishing agroforestry systems (Taungya, coffee shade systems), creating sustainable livelihoods and verified carbon offsets.

### B. Kyoto Protocol Architecture & Flexible Mechanisms
1. **CDM Ineligibility:**
   - **Question:** *"_______ projects are not eligible under CDM."*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_quiz3_2020.pdf` (Q1a), `EVS_mid2_2018.pdf` (Q1a), `EVS_end_2022.pdf` (Section III, Q11)
   - **Answer:** **Nuclear** (Nuclear power/energy projects were explicitly excluded from CDM under the Marrakech Accords due to proliferation, safety, and nuclear waste disposal concerns).
2. **Annex I vs. Annex II Countries:**
   - **Question:** *"Differentiate between Annex-1 and Annex-2 countries."*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_quiz3_2020.pdf` (Q3b), `EVS_mid2_2018.pdf` (Q5b), `EVS_end_2023.pdf` (Section II, Q7)
   - **Answer:**
     - **Annex I:** Developed industrialized OECD nations and Economies in Transition (EITs, like Russia and Eastern European states) that committed to binding emission reduction targets under the Kyoto Protocol.
     - **Annex II:** A sub-group of Annex I countries (only OECD members, excluding EIT countries). Annex II nations are legally obligated to provide financial resources and technology transfer to developing (Non-Annex I) nations to assist in climate mitigation and adaptation.
3. **Kyoto Protocol Flexible Market Mechanisms:**
   - **Question:** *"Explain any 2 market-based flexible mechanisms to reduce emissions under Kyoto Protocol" / "Differentiate between JI and CDM / Emission Trading and JI."*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_end_2023.pdf` (Section IV, Q6), `EVS_mid2_2018.pdf` (Q5c), `EVS_end_2022.pdf` (Section VII, Qe), `EVS_end_2019.pdf` (Part III, Q2)
   - **Answer:**
     - **1. Clean Development Mechanism (CDM):** An Annex I country invests in sustainable, emission-reducing projects in a Non-Annex I (developing) country, receiving **CERs** to meet domestic compliance targets at lower marginal abatement cost.
     - **2. Joint Implementation (JI):** An Annex I country invests in an emission-reduction project in another Annex I country (typically in Eastern Europe/EITs), earning **Emission Reduction Units (ERUs)**.
     - **3. International Emissions Trading (IET):** Annex I countries that reduce emissions below their assigned targets can sell surplus **Assigned Amount Units (AAUs)** to other Annex I countries exceeding their caps.
4. **Kyoto Protocol vs. Paris Agreement:**
   - **Question:** *"What is Kyoto Protocol? Differentiate between Kyoto Protocol and Paris Agreement."*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_end_2023.pdf` (Section II, Q5), `EVS_end_2022.pdf` (Section VII, Qb)
   - **Answer:**
     - **Kyoto Protocol (1997/2005):** Top-down architecture imposing legally binding numerical emission reduction targets only on developed nations (Annex I), with no emission obligations for major developing emitters (China, India).
     - **Paris Agreement (2015):** Bottom-up consensus architecture requiring all 196 nation parties (both developed and developing) to submit voluntary, non-punitive **Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)** with a collective goal to limit warming well below $2^\circ\text{C}$ (targeting $1.5^\circ\text{C}$).

### C. Ocean Chemistry, Iron Fertilization & Novel Materials
1. **John Martin's Ocean Iron Hypothesis:**
   - **Question:** *"Explain Iron Hypothesis."*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_mid2_2018.pdf` (Q9b), `EVS_end_2019.pdf` (Part IV, Q1a)
   - **Answer:** Oceanographer John Martin hypothesized that in High-Nutrient, Low-Chlorophyll (HNLC) ocean regions (e.g., Southern Ocean, Equatorial Pacific), primary biological productivity is limited not by major macronutrients (nitrogen/phosphorus) but by the micronutrient **bioavailable iron ($Fe$)**. Adding iron stimulates massive diatom phytoplankton blooms, absorbing dissolved $CO_2$. When these diatoms die, a fraction sinks into the deep abyss, sequestering carbon for centuries (*"Give me half a tanker of iron, and I'll give you an ice age"*).
2. **Ecological Hazards of Ocean Iron / Urea Fertilization:**
   - **Questions:**
     - *"What are the effects of Urea fertilization?"* (`EVS_quiz3_2020.pdf` Q4b, `EVS_mid2_2018.pdf` Q7b)
     - *"Explain the negative impacts of ocean fertilization of Sulu Sea."* (`EVS_end_2023.pdf` Section III, Q6)
   - **Answer:**
     - **Toxigenic Algae & Neurotoxins:** Fertilization frequently stimulates opportunistic toxic diatoms (e.g. *Pseudo-nitzschia*), which produce lethal **domoic acid** (amnesic shellfish poisoning) and saxitoxin, poisoning marine trophic chains (fish, sea lions, cetaceans).
     - **Deep Water Anoxia:** Sinking biomass is decomposed by aerobic heterotrophic bacteria, exhausting dissolved oxygen and creating widespread sub-surface **hypoxic/anoxic dead zones**.
     - **Potent GHG Generation:** Anoxic decomposition drives denitrification and methanogenesis, producing nitrous oxide ($N_2O$, GWP=265) and methane ($CH_4$, GWP=28), offsetting or completely reversing the climate benefit.
     - **Sulu Sea Case:** Commercial rogue iron-dumping operations off the Philippines triggered illegal, unverified carbon credit claims and severely disrupted local coral reef fisheries.
3. **Ocean Acidification & Sensitivity to Small pH Shifts:**
   - **Questions:**
     - *"What are the effects of Ocean Acidification?"* (`EVS_quiz3_2020.pdf` Q4a)
     - *"Why be concerned with a small change in Ocean pH?"* (`EVS_end_2023.pdf` Section II, Q12)
   - **Answer:**
     - Atmospheric $CO_2$ dissolves into seawater: $CO_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 \rightleftharpoons H^+ + HCO_3^-$. The surplus $H^+$ ions react with available carbonate ions: $H^+ + CO_3^{2-} \rightleftharpoons HCO_3^-$.
     - **Logarithmic Scale:** pH is $-\log_{10}[H^+]$. A seemingly small drop of 0.1 pH units represents an unprecedented **~30% increase in hydrogen ion concentration** $[H^+]$.
     - **Carbonate Undersaturation:** Consuming $CO_3^{2-}$ reduces the mineral saturation state ($\Omega$) of calcite and aragonite below saturation ($\Omega < 1$), dissolving pteropod shells, coral reef skeletons, and larval shellfish shells.
4. **Novacem vs. Portland Cement:**
   - **Questions:**
     - *"Explain Novacem as ecofriendly cement."* (`EVS_end_2019.pdf` Part IV, Q1d, `EVS_mid2_2018.pdf` Q8b)
     - *"Difference between Portland and Novacem cement."* (`EVS_end_2023.pdf` Section III, Q7)
   - **Answer:**
     - **Portland Cement:** Produced by heating limestone ($CaCO_3$) to $1,450^\circ\text{C}$ ($CaCO_3 \to CaO + CO_2$), releasing massive decarbonation emissions plus fuel burning, emitting $\sim 0.8–1.0\text{ tonne } CO_2$ per tonne cement produced.
     - **Novacem:** Based on magnesium silicates ($Mg_4Si_2O_5(OH)_4$) processed at lower calcination temperatures ($650^\circ\text{C}$). The magnesium oxide ($MgO$) reacts with atmospheric $CO_2$ and water during curing to form stable magnesium carbonates ($MgO + CO_2 \to MgCO_3$), sequestering up to **0.6 tonnes of $CO_2$ per tonne of cement produced** (a net carbon-negative material).
5. **Enhanced Coal Bed Methane (ECBM) Storage:**
   - **Question:** *"Carbon storage in coal beds."*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_end_2019.pdf` (Part IV, Q1c)
   - **Answer:** Supercritical $CO_2$ is injected into unminable deep coal seams. The coal matrix has a preferential adsorption affinity for $CO_2$ over methane by a factor of 2:1. $CO_2$ adsorbs onto the micropores, displacing and recovering trapped natural gas ($CH_4$) while locking $CO_2$ permanently in the geological seam. However, accidental fugitive leakage of methane carries a severe climate risk ($72\times$ GWP over 20 years).

# 4. Lecture 11: Negative Emission Technologies (NETs), DAC & Abatement

### A. Core Mechanisms & Technologies
1. **Blue Carbon Habitat Restoration:**
   - **Questions:**
     - *"What is Blue carbon?"* (`EVS_end_2023.pdf` Section I, Q1g)
     - *"Advantages of blue carbon habitat restoration [3 points]."* (`EVS_end_2022.pdf` Section IX, Qe)
   - **Answer:**
     - **Definition:** The carbon captured and stored by coastal and marine vegetated ecosystems, primarily tidal salt marshes, mangrove forests, and seagrass meadows.
     - **Three Advantages:**
       1. **Exceptional Sequestration Density:** Sequester carbon 3 to 5 times (and up to 40 times faster per unit area) than mature terrestrial upland forests, locking organic carbon in anoxic, waterlogged sediments for millennia without saturation.
       2. **Storm and Flood Buffering:** Coastal roots and canopies dissipate wave energy, protecting vulnerable coastal human settlements from storm surges, hurricanes, and coastal erosion.
       3. **Ecological Nursery Grounds:** Serve as vital breeding and feeding nurseries for commercial fisheries, crustaceans, and marine wildlife.
2. **Cattle Enteric Methane Mitigation via Seaweed:**
   - **Question:** *"Methane reduction from livestock can be achieved by adding ________ to cattle feed."*
   - **Sources:** `EVS_end_2022.pdf` (Section III, Q15)
   - **Answer:** **Seaweed** (Specifically the red marine macroalga *Asparagopsis taxiformis*).
     - **Mechanism:** Contains bioactive halogenated compounds (principally **bromoform**, $CHBr_3$) that bind to and inhibit the enzymatic active site of cobamide-dependent methyl-coenzyme M reductase in methanogenic archaea within the rumen, reducing bovine enteric methane emissions by up to $80–90\%$ at feed inclusion rates under $1\%$.
3. **Diamonds from Direct Air Capture (Aether Diamonds):**
   - **Questions:**
     - *"Give an example of a carbon capturing company."* (`EVS_end_2022.pdf` Section VI, Qc)
     - *"Diamonds from air pollution / Explain the process of diamond manufacturing by Aether."* (`EVS_end_2022.pdf` Section X, Qb; `EVS_end_2023.pdf` Section III, Q5)
   - **Answer:**
     - **Companies:** Climeworks, Carbon Engineering, Global Thermostat.
     - **Aether Diamond Manufacturing Process:**
       1. **Capture:** Atmospheric $CO_2$ is captured directly from ambient air using DAC technology (e.g., Climeworks solid amine sorbents).
       2. **Synthesis:** The captured $CO_2$ is reacted with green hydrogen ($H_2$, produced by solar/wind water electrolysis) via the Sabatier methanation reaction ($CO_2 + 4H_2 \to CH_4 + 2H_2O$) to produce ultra-pure methane ($CH_4$).
       3. **Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD):** The synthetic $CH_4$ is injected into vacuum plasma CVD diamond reactors heated to thousands of degrees. Microwave plasma cracks the gas into carbon radicals that crystallize atom-by-atom onto a diamond seed crystal, growing gem-grade diamonds while permanently sequestering carbon.
4. **Enhanced Rock Weathering:**
   - **Key Concept (Lecture 11 Syllabus):**
     - Accelerating natural geological silicate weathering by pulverizing olivine ($Mg_2SiO_4$) or basalt rocks into micro-particles ($<50\,\mu m$) and applying them across agricultural croplands.
     - **Reaction:**
       $$Mg_2SiO_4 + 4CO_2 + 4H_2O \to 2Mg^{2+} + 4HCO_3^- + H_4SiO_4$$
     - Converts atmospheric $CO_2$ into stable dissolved aqueous bicarbonate ($HCO_3^-$) that drains via river basins to the ocean, locking carbon in marine reservoirs for $>100,000\text{ years}$.
5. **Biochar Pyrolysis:**
   - **Key Concept (Lecture 11 Syllabus):**
     - Thermochemical decomposition of agricultural waste/biomass in the total absence of oxygen ($400^\circ\text{C}–700^\circ\text{C}$).
     - Converts labile plant carbon into highly recalcitrant polycyclic aromatic carbon (biochar) with soil mean residence times exceeding $1,000\text{ years}$.
     - Sequestration potential: Up to **4.8 Gt $CO_2$/year** globally, simultaneously improving soil cation exchange capacity (CEC) and moisture retention.
6. **Thermodynamic Minimum Work of Direct Air Capture (Sherwood Plot):**
   - **Key Concept (Lecture 11 Syllabus):**
     - Ambient air $CO_2$ is highly dilute ($\sim 420\text{ ppm} = 0.042\%$), compared to industrial flue gases ($10–15\%$).
     - From the Second Law of Thermodynamics, minimum separation work is given by:
       $$W_{\min} = -RT \left[ \ln x_{CO_2} + \frac{1 - x_{CO_2}}{x_{CO_2}} \ln(1 - x_{CO_2}) \right]$$
     - Because concentration is $\sim 300\times$ more dilute, extracting 1 tonne of $CO_2$ from ambient air requires substantially higher thermodynamic free energy and massive contactor air volumes compared to point-source flue gas capture.
