MechanismsEST Mid 1 2024, EVS Mid 2 2018
3. Why do volcanic eruptions become more frequent when glaciers melt?
Model Answer: Continental ice sheets exert immense weight compressing the Earth's lithosphere. As glaciers melt rapidly, this mass is removed (isostatic rebound). The resulting lithospheric depressurization lowers the melting point of mantle rock and allows trapped magma chambers to expand and gas bubbles to nucleate, sharply accelerating explosive volcanic eruption frequencies.
MechanismsEVS End Sem 2023, EST Mid 1 2024
4. How do Hypercanes differ from present-day hurricanes, and how do they impact Earth's Ozone layer?
Model Answer:
  • Difference: Normal hurricanes require water $\ge 26.5^\circ ext{C}$, reach $\sim 300 ext{ km/h}$, and are confined to the troposphere ($\sim 15 ext{ km}$). Hypercanes require ocean waters reaching $\sim 50^\circ ext{C}$, reach extreme winds $>800 ext{ km/h}$, and have vertical storm columns penetrating $40 ext{–}50 ext{ km}$ into the stratosphere.
  • Ozone Impact: The column lofts colossal amounts of water vapor and sea-salt aerosol chlorine radicals directly into the stratosphere, which catalytically break down stratospheric ozone ($O_3$), destroying Earth's UV-protective layer.
DefinitionsEST Mid 1 2024, EVS Mid 2019
5. Define: (a) Tipping Point, (b) Gray Goo, (c) Global Terrestrial Stilling, (d) Nuclear Winter
Model Answer:
  • Tipping Point: A critical threshold where a tiny additional perturbation triggers a self-reinforcing, non-linear, and irreversible regime shift in the Earth system.
  • Gray Goo: An existential disaster in molecular nanotechnology where synthetic self-replicating nanobots multiply out of control, consuming all planetary carbon into copies of themselves.
  • Global Terrestrial Stilling: A multi-decadal decrease in near-surface wind speeds (~0.3 m/s drop per decade) caused by increased land surface roughness (urbanization, afforestation) and reduced equator-to-pole temperature gradients.
  • Nuclear Winter: Global cooling caused by firestorms after nuclear detonations; black carbon smoke is lofted into the stratosphere, blocking sunlight for months, freezing continents, and halting photosynthesis.
MechanismsEVS End Sem 2023
6. Why is melting of Arctic summer ice considered the single greatest planetary threat?
Model Answer: Melting white sea ice (albedo 0.6–0.85) exposes dark ocean water (albedo 0.06), absorbing $>90\%$ of solar radiation. This ice-albedo positive feedback causes polar amplification, disrupting the polar jet stream, destabilizing Greenland glaciers, and triggering subsea permafrost thaw that risks venting massive subterranean methane hydrates.

2. Carbon Saturation in Climax Mature Forests

"In mature forests, all gross primary productivity is either used up in _____ or returned to soil as _____ with no net addition to standing biomass."

Model Answer: Plant / autotrophic respiration; litterfall / dead organic debris. In mature forests, photosynthetic gains equal plant respiration plus heterotrophic litter decomposition ($GPP pprox R_{ ext{auto}} + R_{ ext{hetero}}$), reaching carbon saturation.

3. Soil vs. Terrestrial Vegetation Carbon Reservoir Ratio

"Soils store about _____ times as much carbon as does living terrestrial vegetation."

Core ConceptsEVS Quiz 3 2020, End Sem 2019, Quiz 4 2020
4. Differentiate between Afforestation and Reforestation
Model Answer:
  • Afforestation: Planting trees on land that historically has not contained forest cover for at least 50 years under UNFCCC rules (e.g. barren lands, degraded drylands).
  • Reforestation: Re-establishing trees on land that historically supported forests but was recently converted or cleared through deforestation, logging, or fire.
Applied EcologyEVS Quiz 3 2020, End Sem 2019
5. Why is Agroforestry an attractive option for carbon mitigation?
Model Answer:
  1. Deep Subsoil Storage: Tree root networks deposit carbon into deep subsoil horizons resistant to oxidation and tilling disturbance.
  2. Food Security Synergy: Combines trees with crops/livestock without removing fertile agricultural land from food production.
  3. Fertilizer & $N_2O$ Reduction: Leguminous trees fix atmospheric nitrogen, enhancing soil fertility while lowering synthetic nitrogen fertilizer requirements.
Case StudiesEVS Quiz 3 2020, End Sem 2019
6. Describe any one International Carbon Sequestration Project
Model Answer:
  • INFAPRO Project (Sabah, Malaysia): Partnership between Innoprise Corp and the Dutch FACE Foundation. Rehabilitated 25,000 hectares of logged, degraded Dipterocarp rainforest through enrichment planting with native wildings, sequestering millions of tonnes of carbon over a 60-year concession while restoring endangered wildlife habitats.
  • (Alternative) Plan Vivo / Scolel'te Project (Chiapas, Mexico): Community agroforestry initiative where Mayan farmers manage native timber (cedar, mahogany) and shade-grown coffee, selling verified carbon credits to support rural livelihoods.

Lecture 10: Kyoto Architecture, CERs, Ocean Acidification & Geoengineering

Fill in the BlankEVS Quiz 3 2020, Mid 2 2018, End 2022
1. Projects Explicitly Ineligible under CDM

"_____ projects are not eligible under CDM."

Model Answer: Nuclear (or Nuclear energy/power projects). Explicitly excluded under the Marrakech Accords due to proliferation hazards, safety, and nuclear waste storage concerns.
Comparative AnalysisEVS Quiz 3 2020, Mid 2 2018, End 2023
2. Differentiate between Annex I and Annex II Countries
Model Answer:
  • Annex I: Industrialized OECD countries plus Economies in Transition (EITs like Russia and Eastern Europe) that committed to binding emission reduction caps under Kyoto.
  • Annex II: A subset of Annex I (OECD members only, excluding EITs) legally mandated to provide financial resources and technology transfer to Non-Annex I developing nations.
Flexible MechanismsEVS End Sem 2023, Mid 2 2018, End 2022
3. Explain the Market-Based Flexible Mechanisms under the Kyoto Protocol
Model Answer:
  1. Clean Development Mechanism (CDM): Annex I countries invest in green emission-reduction projects in Non-Annex I developing nations, generating Certified Emission Reductions (CERs) ($1 ext{ CER} = 1 ext{ t } CO_2 ext{e}$).
  2. Joint Implementation (JI): Annex I countries invest in emission-reduction projects in other Annex I countries (mainly EITs), earning Emission Reduction Units (ERUs).
  3. International Emissions Trading (IET): Annex I nations trade assigned surplus allowance units (AAUs) directly among themselves.
Marine ChemistryEVS Quiz 3 2020, End Sem 2023
4. Ocean Acidification & Logarithmic Sensitivity

"What are the effects of ocean acidification, and why be concerned with a small change in ocean pH?"

Model Answer: Dissolved $CO_2$ produces carbonic acid ($CO_2 + H_2O ightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 ightleftharpoons H^+ + HCO_3^-$). Surplus $H^+$ reacts with carbonate: $H^+ + CO_3^{2-} ightleftharpoons HCO_3^-$.
Because pH is a logarithmic scale ($-\log_{10}[H^+]$), a 0.1 unit drop represents a ~30% increase in $[H^+]$. Consuming carbonate drops the saturation state ($\Omega < 1$) of aragonite and calcite, dissolving shells of corals, pteropods, and mollusks.
Geoengineering HazardsEVS Mid 2 2018, End 2019, End 2023
5. John Martin's Ocean Iron Hypothesis & Ecological Hazards of Ocean Fertilization (Sulu Sea)
Model Answer:
  • Iron Hypothesis: Iron is the limiting micronutrient in High-Nutrient Low-Chlorophyll (HNLC) oceans. Seeding iron triggers diatom blooms that sequester carbon to deep sediments ("Give me half a tanker of iron, and I'll give you an ice age").
  • Ecological Hazards (Sulu Sea): Stimulates toxic diatoms (Pseudo-nitzschia) that produce lethal domoic acid (amnesic shellfish poisoning); sinking biomass decomposition creates deep-water anoxia; anoxic decay emits potent GHGs ($N_2O$ and $CH_4$).
Cleantech MaterialsEVS End 2023, End 2019, Mid 2 2018
6. Difference between Portland Cement and Novacem Cement
Model Answer:
  • Portland Cement: Made from limestone ($CaCO_3$) calcined at $1,450^\circ ext{C}$; releases decarbonation and fuel emissions of $0.8 ext{–}1.0 ext{ t } CO_2$ per ton cement produced.
  • Novacem Cement: Uses magnesium silicates calcined at $650^\circ ext{C}$. Absorbs $CO_2$ during curing ($MgO + CO_2 o MgCO_3$), sequestering $0.6 ext{ t } CO_2$ per ton produced (net carbon negative).

Lecture 11: Negative Emission Technologies, DAC & Cleantech Solutions

Core ConceptsEVS End Sem 2022, End Sem 2023
1. Blue Carbon Habitat Restoration & 3 Key Advantages
Model Answer: Blue carbon is carbon stored by coastal marine ecosystems (mangroves, tidal salt marshes, seagrass meadows).
Three Key Advantages:
  1. No Saturation: Sequestration rate is 3 to 5 times (and up to 40x per unit area) higher than terrestrial forests; sediments remain anoxic and saline, locking carbon for millennia without saturating.
  2. Coastal Protection: Buffers shorelines by attenuating wave energy from storm surges and cyclones.
  3. Marine Habitat: Serves as critical breeding and nursery grounds for commercial fish and crustaceans.
Fill in the BlankEVS End Sem 2022
2. Livestock Enteric Methane Mitigation via Seaweed

"Methane reduction from livestock can be achieved by adding _____ to cattle feed."

Model Answer: Seaweed (specifically the red macroalga Asparagopsis taxiformis). Contains bromoform ($CHBr_3$) which inhibits methyl-coenzyme M reductase in rumen methanogenic archaea, reducing methane emissions by $80 ext{–}90\%$.
Exotic CleantechEVS End Sem 2022, End Sem 2023
3. Diamonds from Air Pollution (Aether Diamonds Manufacturing Process)
Model Answer:
  1. Capture: Ambient $CO_2$ is captured directly from air via Direct Air Capture (Climeworks).
  2. Methanation: $CO_2$ is combined with green hydrogen ($H_2$) via the Sabatier reaction ($CO_2 + 4H_2 o CH_4 + 2H_2O$) to produce high-purity methane.
  3. Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD): Methane is cracked in microwave plasma CVD reactors, depositing carbon atom-by-atom to grow gem-grade diamonds.
Chemical ReactionLecture 11 Core Syllabus
4. Enhanced Silicate Rock Weathering (Olivine Reaction)
Model Answer: Spreading micro-pulverized olivine on agricultural soils reacts with soil rainwater and carbonic acid: $$Mg_2SiO_4 + 4CO_2 + 4H_2O o 2Mg^{2+} + 4HCO_3^- + H_4SiO_4$$ Converts gaseous $CO_2$ into stable aqueous bicarbonate ($HCO_3^-$) washed to the ocean, sequestering carbon for $>100,000 ext{ years}$.

📋 Mock Exam 2: Model Answers & Rubric

Section I:

1. Glacial Isostatic Rebound: Mass unloading causes mantle depressurization, lowering rock melting points and venting magma chambers.

2. Hypercane Ozone Decay: $50 ext{ km}$ vertical column injects water vapor and chlorine radicals into stratosphere, catalytically destroying $O_3$.

3. Stilling: Surface wind slowdown (~0.3 m/s drop per decade) due to surface roughness and reduced equator-to-pole thermal gradient.

4. Sherwood Rule: Minimum work $W_{\min} = -RT[\ln x + rac{1-x}{x}\ln(1-x)]$. Atmospheric $CO_2$ is dilute ($420 ext{ ppm} = 0.042\%$), requiring $\sim 3 imes$ higher theoretical energy and massive contactor fans.

5. Seaweed: Active compound is bromoform ($CHBr_3$); inhibits methyl-coenzyme M reductase in rumen archaea, cutting methane by $80 ext{–}90\%$.


Section II:

1. CDM: Annex I $ o$ Non-Annex I (CERs). JI: Annex I $ o$ Annex I (ERUs). IET: Trading AAUs among Annex I nations.

2. Portland: Limestone ($CaCO_3$) calcined at $1450^\circ ext{C}$; emits $+0.8 ext{–}1.0 ext{ t } CO_2/ ext{t}$. Novacem: Magnesium silicate calcined at $650^\circ ext{C}$; absorbs $CO_2$ during curing, netting $-0.6 ext{ t } CO_2/ ext{t}$.

3. Iron Hypothesis: Fe micronutrient in HNLC ocean stimulates diatom sink. Enhanced Weathering: Micro-pulverized olivine on soil: $Mg_2SiO_4 + 4CO_2 + 4H_2O o 2Mg^{2+} + 4HCO_3^- + H_4SiO_4$.


Section III:

1. Aether Pipeline: (1) DAC CO2 capture; (2) Sabatier methanation ($CO_2 + 4H_2 o CH_4 + 2H_2O$); (3) Microwave plasma CVD diamond reactor. DAC companies: Climeworks (solid amine) & Carbon Engineering (liquid KOH).

2. Black Swan: Unpredictable outlier with extreme impact, rationalized in hindsight. 3 Tipping elements: Arctic ice (albedo drop), WAIS (marine ice sheet instability commits 3–5m sea level rise), Amazon (loss of biotic pump converts to scrubland, venting 100 Gt C).

3. Blue Carbon: Coastal wetlands build soil vertically in saline anoxic water without microbial decay. Biochar: $400 ext{–}700^\circ ext{C}$ anoxic pyrolysis; $>1000 ext{ yr}$ soil residence time; up to $4.8 ext{ Gt } CO_2/ ext{yr}$ potential.
